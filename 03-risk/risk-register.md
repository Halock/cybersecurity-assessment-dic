## 1. Purpose

This risk register summarizes the principal security risks identified during the cybersecurity assessment.

The register provides a structured method for documenting identified risks, assessing their likelihood and potential impact, and prioritizing security improvements.

> **Portfolio Note:** This is a sanitized public version. Sensitive operational information, confidential evidence, specific infrastructure details, and exploitable security information have been excluded.

---

## 2. Risk Assessment Method

Risk was calculated using:

**Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact**

### Likelihood Scale

| Score | Description    |
| ----: | -------------- |
|     1 | Rare           |
|     2 | Unlikely       |
|     3 | Possible       |
|     4 | Likely         |
|     5 | Almost Certain |

### Impact Scale

| Score | Description |
| ----: | ----------- |
|     1 | Negligible  |
|     2 | Minor       |
|     3 | Moderate    |
|     4 | Major       |
|     5 | Critical    |

### Risk Classification

| Score | Risk Level |
| ----: | ---------- |
|   1–4 | Low        |
|   5–9 | Medium     |
| 10–16 | High       |
| 17–25 | Critical   |

# 5. High-Risk Findings

## F-01 — Public-Facing Authentication Exposure

**Risk Score:** 12
**Risk Level:** High

### Risk Description

Public-facing authentication services can increase exposure to credential-related attacks, phishing, impersonation, and unauthorized-access attempts.

### Potential Impact

Potential consequences may include:

* Unauthorized access
* Credential compromise
* Information exposure
* Service disruption
* Reputational impact

### Recommended Treatment

* Strengthen authentication controls.
* Monitor externally exposed assets.
* Conduct regular security reviews.
* Improve phishing awareness.
* Monitor for credential exposure.

---

## F-02 — Institutional Information Exposure

**Risk Score:** 12
**Risk Level:** High

### Risk Description

Excessive publicly available institutional information can assist threat actors with reconnaissance, impersonation, and social-engineering activities.

### Potential Impact

Potential consequences may include:

* Targeted phishing
* Executive impersonation
* Social engineering
* Information gathering

### Recommended Treatment

* Review publicly available institutional information.
* Minimize unnecessary sensitive metadata.
* Strengthen executive security awareness.
* Conduct periodic OSINT reviews.

---

## F-07 — Cybersecurity Workforce Capacity

**Risk Score:** 16
**Risk Level:** High

### Risk Description

Limited availability of specialized cybersecurity personnel can reduce the organization's ability to perform continuous monitoring, threat hunting, incident response, and security improvement activities.

### Potential Impact

Potential consequences may include:

* Increased response times
* Analyst workload
* Reduced threat-hunting capacity
* Increased dependence on external expertise
* Delayed remediation

### Recommended Treatment

* Strengthen cybersecurity recruitment.
* Develop specialist training programs.
* Support professional certifications.
* Establish practical threat-hunting exercises.
* Improve workforce retention.
* Develop partnerships for cybersecurity skills development.

# 9. Risk Monitoring

Risk should be reviewed periodically and updated when:

* New threats are identified.
* New systems are introduced.
* Significant infrastructure changes occur.
* Security incidents occur.
* Major vulnerabilities are discovered.
* Organizational responsibilities change.
* Regulatory requirements change.

A maintained risk register supports continuous security governance and informed decision-making.
